# Plutus — Feature Backlog & Scope Discipline

Derived from two comprehensive HRMS/payroll feature taxonomies (July 2026), **triaged against the Plutus thesis rather than adopted wholesale.**

## How to use this file

Those source taxonomies describe a *horizontal* enterprise HRMS — roughly 800 line items spanning ATS, LMS, talent management, health and safety, asset tracking, travel booking and more. Plutus's entire moat is the opposite shape: **vertical depth in Nigerian statutory compliance.** A finance buyer chooses Plutus because its PAYE is provably correct under the 2026 Act, not because it also books flights.

So this file is a **triaged backlog, not a roadmap commitment.** Read it when scoping, prioritising, or deciding whether an incoming feature request belongs in the product. The `Not doing` section is as load-bearing as the rest — scope discipline is the strategy here, and a pre-revenue product that scopes 38 modules ships none of them.

**Standing rule:** nothing in this file outranks phase 1 in the build roadmap. The compliance engine and its golden tests come first regardless of how compelling anything below looks.

## 1. Correctness gaps — highest priority

These are places where the current engine spec is **incomplete in ways that produce wrong money**. They outrank every feature in this document and belong in phases 1–3. Each needs golden tests alongside the cases in `nigeria-statutory-compliance.md` §12.

### Proration

Not currently specified anywhere, and it applies to a large share of real pay runs:

- **New hire mid-period** — partial month from start date. **Built.**
- **Termination mid-period** — partial month to last working day. **Built:** Final Settlement now pays prorated regular basic/housing/transport for the days between the employee's last regular pay run and their last working day (sourced from the auto-logged status-change history), pensionable and NHF-able like ordinary pay, combined into one cumulative-PAYE calculation together with leave payout and gratuity — previously a silent gap where a terminated employee was never paid for days worked in their unfinished final period. Rent relief is not re-prorated for that stub period (disclosed simplification).
- ~~**Mid-month salary change**~~ — split period across two rates. **Built:** `employee_compensation_history` (mirrors `employee_status_history`'s auto-logged-trigger pattern exactly — old/new basic/housing/transport, `changed_at` never client-written) supplies the effective-dated data this needed. Pay-run creation finds the earliest compensation change within the period, computes a signed `salary_change_adjustment_kobo` the same daily-rate way as new-hire proration (positive for a raise, since the pre-change days were over-credited at the new rate; negative for a pay cut, since they were under-credited), and folds it into gross/chargeable income alongside the run's other proration deductions. RLS on the history table is admin/payroll_manager only, narrower than status history, matching the existing salary-masking sensitivity rule. A second compensation change within the same single period isn't given its own segment — only the earliest change is treated as the two-way split boundary — a disclosed simplification for a genuinely rare case (two raises inside one pay period).
- **Unpaid leave and absence** — partial-period deduction. **Built.**

Proration interacts directly with **cumulative PAYE**: a prorated month changes year-to-date chargeable income, which changes the marginal band the next month falls into. Getting proration right but recomputing cumulatively wrong (or vice versa) produces plausible-looking payslips that are wrong by material amounts. Decide and document the proration basis — calendar days, working days, or a 30-day convention — because the three give different answers and the choice must be consistent across the engine.

### ~~Net-to-gross (gross-up)~~

Currently the engine only computes gross → net. Gross-up is common in Nigerian senior and expatriate contracts where the employer bears the tax. It requires **iterative solving**, because PAYE is progressive: the tax depends on gross, and gross depends on the tax. Implement as a converging solve with an explicit tolerance and iteration cap, and test it at band boundaries where a small gross change crosses a rate step.

**Built (2026-07-31), scoped to lump sums.** `solveLumpSumGrossForNetKobo()` bisects to the exact minimal gross whose net is ≥ the target, bounded by a 128-iteration cap. Net-of-PAYE is provably monotonically non-decreasing in gross (every band's marginal rate is below 100%, and rounding of a non-decreasing input is itself non-decreasing), so bisection is guaranteed correct — no false convergence, including across a band boundary. `deriveGrossedUpLumpSumPayslip()` wraps the solve and re-derives the payslip from the solved gross, carrying the same non-pensionable/non-NHF/non-NSITF treatment as any other lump sum. Golden-tested at the ₦12,000,000 (18%→21%) boundary and deep in the top 25% band. Wired into the Bonus pay-run flow as a "gross-up" toggle (amounts entered are net; employer bears the tax).

**Regular-package gross-up: built (2026-09-12).** `solveRegularPackageGrossUp()` extends the same bisection to an employee's basic/housing/transport package, resolving the "which component absorbs the increase" question the way the arrears rule-version question was resolved — as an explicit, disclosed product decision rather than a guess: **distribute the increase proportionally**, preserving the employee's existing basic:housing:transport ratio. Chosen over concentrating the raise in basic alone (which would inflate the pension/NHF bases, both driven off basic, more than a real package revision typically would) and over concentrating it in a non-pensionable component (which would let a "gross-up" quietly shrink pension exposure). Rent relief doesn't affect the choice — it depends only on `annualRentPaidKobo`, never on the package's internal split. Monotonicity holds by the same argument as the lump-sum solver (pension + NHF + top PAYE band together stay well under 100%), so bisection converges correctly, including across the ₦12,000,000 boundary. Golden-tested for ratio preservation, band-boundary cost, and non-decreasing pension/NHF exposure. **Not built:** wiring this into pay-run creation UI the way the lump-sum toggle is wired into Bonus — this closes the compliance-engine gap only, not a compensation-change screen.

### Retroactive pay and the rule-version question

**Built, with the rule-version question resolved by product decision — not yet confirmed against a Nigerian tax professional.** If arrears are paid in July 2026 for work performed in December 2025 — under the previous Personal Income Tax Act regime — which rule version applies?

- Taxed in the **period of receipt** (current rules), or
- Attributed to the **period earned** (the rules then in force)?

This was flagged as needing a Nigerian tax professional's input before building, since the answer determines whether `RuleVersion` pinning is per-run or per-earning-period — a foundational, expensive-to-reverse data-model decision. Asked directly, the product decision was **period of receipt**: arrears is taxed under whichever `RuleVersion` the pay run posts under (`deriveLumpSumPayslip(kind: "arrears")`, same cumulative-PAYE mechanism as bonus/13th month), with no per-earning-period `RuleVersion` pinning added. This is disclosed as a decision made without the recommended professional confirmation, not as a resolved statutory question — flag it if a real cross-regime arrears case (a rate change or band edit mid-year) comes up before that confirmation happens.

A separate, undisclosed simplification rides along with this: arrears is treated exactly like bonus/13th month for pension/NHF purposes — never pensionable, never NHF-deductible — even though a real arrears payment (e.g. a late-processed raise) often represents back-pay for what would have been ordinary pensionable basic/housing/transport. Restating the pensionable/NHF position for the periods the shortfall covers isn't attempted. The one thing this build does carry forward correctly is audit context: `payslips.arrears_note`, required per employee at pay-run creation, records which period the arrears relates to and why — so that information isn't lost even though it isn't used to select a different rule version.

### Payroll reversal and correction

The skill establishes payslips and audit events as append-only, but doesn't specify what happens when a finalised run is wrong:

- Reversal of a finalised run — and what that does to **already-remitted** statutory liabilities. **Partially built**, closing the tractable half of what this item originally left open: this build never had a way to record that a remittance happened at all, so a new `statutory_remittances` table (admin/payroll_manager/finance_manager can record one, against a specific pay run's PAYE/pension/NHF/NSITF liability, via `record_statutory_remittance`) gives it one, surfaced on the pay run detail page next to the run's own posted liability per scheme. `reverse_pay_run` now checks it: reversing a run with a recorded remittance against it fails unless the caller explicitly passes `p_acknowledge_remitted = true` (the UI gates this behind a checkbox naming exactly which scheme, how much, and when), naming which scheme(s) and amounts the correction won't undo. This doesn't decide whether the correction is *right* once remitted — it converts a silent gap into a conscious, logged choice, which is as far as this build can go without answering the still-open question below.
- Correcting-entry mechanics through `packages/ledger` (never an edit in place). **Built:** `reverse_pay_run` posts a correcting journal entry with every original posting re-inserted at flipped direction, against the same accounts and amounts. The original postings and payslips are never edited or deleted — append-only, matching the audit-event philosophy.
- Whether a reversal after a filing deadline requires an amended filing, per scheme. **Still open, deliberately.** This is a tax-professional question — decided per scheme, per authority, per how close the run is to its deadline — that guessing at risks telling an employer the wrong thing to do with a regulator. The acknowledgment path above says exactly that instead of pretending to resolve it; it does not attempt an amended-filing workflow of any kind.
- Restoring the side effects a reversed run consumed — loan outstanding balances, and expense/leave/attendance/overtime/leave-encashment rows it marked paid/consumed. **Built**, closing a gap this section originally disclosed and left open: `reverse_pay_run` now runs the identical restoration logic `discard_pay_run_draft` uses for a draft (loan balance and `completed`→`approved` status put back by the amount in `loan_repayments`; expense/leave/overtime/leave-encashment rows reset to `approved` with `paid_pay_run_id` cleared; attendance records' `paid_pay_run_id` cleared), so a reversed run's consumed resources are picked up correctly by whatever run replaces it. Live-verified via a full round-trip against real demo-org data touching all five side-effect types plus a balanced correcting journal entry.

Reversal-vs-remittance awareness is now built; the amended-filing question above remains **the hardest correctness area left in payroll, and the most likely to be discovered in production** — it needs a real tax professional's input per scheme before this build could safely automate or even recommend an answer.

### Payroll locking and finalisation

**Built (2026-09-12), all four stages, deliberately mapped onto the existing schema rather than a status-value rename.** `create_pay_run` for regular (weekly/biweekly/monthly), 13th Month and Bonus runs still inserts with `status = 'draft'` — every side effect (loan repayments, expense/overtime/leave-encashment payouts, unpaid-leave/absence deductions) fires at creation time, so a draft's numbers are real but invisible everywhere until acted on.

- **preview**: needs no schema — it's the payroll/new form itself, computed before `create_pay_run` is ever called.
- **validated**: a genuinely new status value. `validate_pay_run()` (draft → validated) does what `approve_pay_run` used to (the variance-flag acknowledgment gate, now removed and replaced) plus a fresh, hard-stop TIN re-check against current employee records — closing the gap where a TIN could be cleared after `checkTinGate` ran at draft-creation time but before the run finalised. Matches `checkTinGate`'s own rule exactly (presence only, not the unused `tin_valid_to`/`tin_valid_from` expiry columns — inventing a stricter check here alone would be new undisclosed policy, not a re-check of an existing one). `posted_payslips` already read `status = 'posted'` exactly (not `!= 'draft'` — a prior migration moved off that because it silently re-included reversed runs), so a validated-but-unlocked run is correctly still invisible to every report/reconciliation/tax-certificate/GL-export site with no further change needed.
- **locked**: deliberately *not* a fourth status value. At least ten call sites across reports, tax certificates, the dashboard and notifications already read `status = 'posted'` directly against `pay_runs`, not only through the view — renaming that value for zero behavioural gain would mean auditing and touching every one of them. `lock_pay_run()` (validated → locked) is what `approve_pay_run` used to finish with: stamps `status = 'posted'`, `approved_by`, `approved_at`. What's new is a trigger, `core.prevent_rule_version_change_once_locked()`, making `rule_version_id` genuinely immutable once `status` is `'posted'` or `'reversed'` — the "rule-version freezing" this section always meant, enforced in the database rather than by convention (nothing ever offered a way to change it, but nothing stopped a direct `UPDATE` either).
- **paid**: tracked as `disbursed_by`/`disbursed_at` columns, not a status value, for the same reason as locked. `mark_pay_run_paid()` (locked → paid) is admin/payroll_manager/finance_manager-only, the same role set as `record_statutory_remittance`. See "Failed payment tracking and reconciliation" below for the disbursement-file generation this pairs with (added the same day) and what's still open there.

`discard_pay_run_draft` is unchanged and still draft-only — once validated, correcting a run means reversing it, never discarding. `approve_pay_run(uuid, boolean)` is dropped outright (not kept alongside the new functions), so no caller can skip the new TIN re-check by going straight from draft to posted. Final Settlement's pay run remains deliberately excluded from the draft/validate/lock gate entirely — it still posts (locks) immediately, for the same NOT NULL-eligibility-marker reason as before.

### Other calculation scenarios needing golden tests

- Leave encashment and its tax treatment. **Built:** cashing out unused annual leave for money while still employed (distinct from Final Settlement's termination-only leave payout). Taxable, non-pensionable, same daily-rate convention as unpaid leave/overtime/new-hire proration, combined into the run's single cumulative-PAYE calculation (never a separate call). Approval atomically checks and decrements the employee's current balance in one security-definer function (`review_leave_encashment_request`) — an over-request is rejected and rolled back cleanly, never partially applied.
- 13th month / bonus interaction with cumulative PAYE (a bonus can push the year into a higher band). **Built:** a dedicated golden-test block in `payslip.test.ts` pins a 13th-month/bonus payment crossing the ₦12,000,000 boundary, taxed more than a flat single-band estimate would predict.
- Termination payments: gratuity is taxable under the new Act; severance treatment. **Built (gratuity):** a golden test covers a combined leave-payout + gratuity settlement crossing a PAYE band boundary, taxed on top of year-to-date rather than in isolation. Severance treatment specifically remains unaddressed — Final Settlement models gratuity and leave payout, not a distinct severance payment.
- Overtime and shift premiums entering the PAYE base. **Built (overtime, 2026-09-12):** overtime pay is folded into a regular period's chargeable income as a taxable addback and cumulative PAYE recomputed off the combined position (`payroll/new/actions.ts`) — a golden test pins this same shape crossing the ₦12,000,000 boundary, plus confirms overtime contributes nothing to the pension/NHF base. Shift premiums specifically aren't modelled as their own component anywhere in this build (only a flat overtime rate multiplier exists), so there's nothing yet to golden-test there.
- Loan deduction ordering against statutory deductions when net pay would go negative — **decide the priority order explicitly**, since statutory deductions cannot be skipped. **Closed:** the priority is statutory (never skipped) → loans (already capped against remaining net) → benefits (previously unenforced — a benefit enrollment's employee cost is now skipped in its entirety, earliest-enrolled first, if it can't be fully covered by what loans left behind, rather than partially charged). `payslips.net_kobo >= 0` is now also a database check constraint, not just an application-level invariant.

## 2. Genuine platform gaps

Real absences from the current spec, in rough priority order.

### HR core — the part that blocks payroll

Plutus is described as an HR *and* payroll platform, but the HR side is currently thin, and the thinness is not evenly distributed. Some of it is legitimately deferred product surface. Some of it is **data payroll cannot run without**, which makes it phase-2/3 work rather than a later HR module.

**Blocking — build before or alongside payroll runs:**

| Missing | Why payroll breaks without it |
|---|---|
| ~~**Bank account details**~~ | Disbursement files are a documented feature, but nothing in the model holds bank, NUBAN or account name. You cannot pay anyone. This is the sharpest hole. **Built:** `bank_name`/`bank_account_number`/`bank_account_name` on `employees`, captured on the add/edit forms — see also §3's NUBAN check-digit validation, layered on top of this. |
| ~~**Departments / cost centres**~~ | Pay-run scoping already offers "single department", and payroll accounting needs cost-centre allocation. Both reference an entity that doesn't exist. **Built:** an org-scoped `departments` catalog, employees optionally assigned to one, a Department Manager role scoped to their own department's leave/overtime/loans. The cost-centre *allocation* half — ledger postings actually broken down by department — is still open; see "Payroll accounting depth" below. |
| ~~**Branches / locations**~~ | Statutory filing is state-scoped; multi-state employers need locations as records, not free text. **Built:** an org-scoped `branches` catalog (name, state, address), employees optionally assigned to one, with directory search/filter. The `state` field is a work-location record only — it does not drive PAYE routing, which stays keyed off each employee's own `state_of_residence`, per this same table's note that residence/origin/work location are three different things. |
| ~~**Employment type + dates**~~ | Proration needs date of joining and last working day. Contract vs permanent changes statutory treatment. **Built:** `hire_date`, `employment_type` (permanent/contract/intern, descriptive only — not wired into PAYE/pension/NHF, a genuinely open statutory question), and `contract_end_date`, all captured on the employee record and used by proration/contract-expiry alerting elsewhere in this document. |
| ~~**Employee master data**~~ | DOB (pension eligibility), nationality (expatriate treatment), and state of residence vs state of origin vs work location — three different things, and PAYE routes on residence. **Built:** `date_of_birth` and `nationality` are captured, plus a residential address distinct from both `state_of_residence` (drives PAYE) and work location (the branch's `state`). DOB isn't yet wired into any pension-age eligibility rule and nationality isn't yet wired into expatriate PAYE treatment — both remain open statutory questions this build doesn't guess at, same disclosed gap as employment type above. |
| ~~**Lifecycle state**~~ | Active / probation / suspended / on unpaid leave / exited determines whether an employee is included in a run at all, and how they're prorated. **Built: suspended.** `employees.status` now accepts active/suspended/terminated (was active/terminated only). A suspended employee is excluded from regular pay-run creation via the same `status = 'active'` filter every other non-active status already used — zero query changes needed — but unlike terminated, it does not revoke self-service access or trigger Final Settlement eligibility, since both of those gates key specifically on `'terminated'`. Probation is a separate existing flag (not a status value), already excluded from payroll math by design. "On unpaid leave" has no dedicated status — it's handled per-period via unpaid leave requests reducing pay, not as an employee-level state. |

**Near-blocking — needed for the product to be usable, not for the arithmetic to be correct.** All six items below are now built, each with a disclosed simplification — none of them is a claim of statutory or architectural completeness, just that the specific listed gap is closed:

~~Employment contracts and contract-expiry alerting~~ **(built: employment_type — permanent/contract/intern, descriptive only, not wired into PAYE/pension/NHF, since contract-vs-permanent statutory treatment is a genuinely open question — plus contract_end_date, surfaced as an Expired/Ends soon/Active contract badge in the employee directory; "alerting" here is a badge checked on page load, not a scheduled push notification, since this app has no background job runner to fire one)** · ~~employee documents repository~~ **(built: a private Supabase Storage bucket, path-scoped `{org_id}/{employee_id}/{filename}` and RLS-policed via `storage.foldername`, plus an `employee_documents` metadata table for listing. Admin/HR can upload — with an optional free-text `document_type` label, not a structured taxonomy — and delete for any employee; an employee can view and download their own from `/me`. Generic file storage only, no e-signature and no document generation — that's the separate "Document generation" capability above)** · ~~probation tracking and confirmation~~ **(built: probation_end_date + a confirmed flag, surfaced as an Overdue/Ends soon/On probation badge in the employee directory; deliberately not wired into payroll math — Nigerian law doesn't condition PAYE/pension/NHF on probation status)** · ~~organisation chart and reporting hierarchy~~ **(built: a company-wide /org-chart view, cycle-safe — a manager loop can't be created or rendered)** · ~~job grades and salary bands~~ **(built: /job-grades admin CRUD, wired into the employee edit page with a band-compliance check flagging an employee whose annual pay falls outside their assigned grade)** · ~~employee directory search and filtering~~ **(built: search by name plus filter by status/department/branch on the employees list)** · ~~company policies with acknowledgement~~ **(built: admin/HR-authored policy documents, org-wide read + per-employee `policy_acknowledgements`; editing a policy is a timestamp bump, not a versioned document history, which staleness-checks every prior acknowledgement and re-notifies every linked employee to re-acknowledge; status is computed on page load, not a scheduled reminder push, since this app has no background job runner)**.

**Legitimately deferred** — see §4. Recruitment/ATS, performance, learning, talent, engagement, employee relations, health and safety, assets, travel and HR service desk are all real HRMS modules, and none of them is needed for a correct payslip.

The distinction is worth holding onto when scoping. "HR" in the source taxonomies is one undifferentiated 20-module block; for Plutus it splits cleanly into *employee data payroll depends on* (urgent, small, unglamorous) and *HR process modules* (large, deferrable, each a product in its own right). Build the first, defer the second, and don't let the word "HR" blur them together.

### ~~SaaS billing and metering~~ — *the notable one*

The business model is **per-employee-per-month**, but no module meters employees, tracks subscriptions, enforces plan limits, or invoices. This is the gap that stops Plutus taking money. Needs: tenant/company registration and self-serve org onboarding, active-employee metering per billing period, subscription plans and tiers, feature flags for tiered gating, invoicing and payment, trial periods, upgrade/downgrade/cancellation.

Note the metering subtlety: "employees" for billing means *active in the period*, which must reconcile with — but is not identical to — the headcount a pay run processes. Define it precisely or every invoice is arguable.

**Built (2026-07-31).** `core.compute_billable_employee_count()` answers the metering subtlety above directly: it reconstructs each employee's full history of active/suspended intervals from `employee_status_history` (plus their true initial state, since an employee's *first* status was never itself a logged "change") rather than trusting `employees.status` as of now, so a new hire, an exit, or a leave-and-return mid-period is all counted correctly for that period specifically — verified against synthetic histories covering exactly those three cases, including a bug (an employee's earliest interval silently disappearing once they had any logged transition) caught before shipping. `subscription_plans` (two seeded demo tiers — illustrative pricing only), `org_subscriptions` (one per org, starts on a 14-day trial atomically in `create_organization()`), `billing_periods` (written once a period closes, immutable after) and `invoices` (record-only) round it out. `core.close_billing_periods()` runs daily via `pg_cron`: closes any org whose period ended, meters it, issues an invoice, rolls the subscription forward a month. Admin-only **Billing & Subscription** page shows the current running estimate and invoice history with a mark-paid action. **Not built:** self-serve org onboarding/signup flow changes, feature-flag tiered gating (a Growth-plan feature is not actually blocked from a Core-plan org yet — the tiers are priced and named but not enforced), trial-expiry handling beyond the status field, upgrade/downgrade/cancellation UI, and any real payment gateway (Paystack/Stripe) — marking an invoice paid just records that payment happened somewhere else.

### ~~Field-level security and salary masking~~

Currently RLS isolates tenants and roles gate screens, but nothing masks salary within an authorised role. An HR Manager may legitimately need employee records without seeing executive compensation. Given how sensitive payroll is, field-level permissions and salary masking are closer to table stakes than to a premium feature. **Built:** a real database-level mask — a per-employee `salary_masked` boolean, enforced by the `employees_masked` view (`case when salary_masked and not core.has_org_role(...'admin','payroll_manager')`) rather than client-side field hiding — hides compensation and bank details from any non-admin/payroll_manager viewer (in practice, hr_manager) across the employees list and edit page. hr_manager never controls the mask flag itself, since letting HR unmask their own view would defeat it.

### Workflow and approval engine

The prototype has approvals scattered per feature (leave, expenses, loans). A **configurable** engine — multi-level, sequential and parallel, conditional, role- and department-based, with delegation, escalation, reminders and SLA tracking — is a foundational architectural choice. Cheaper to build once early than to retrofit across eight modules later.

**Built (2026-07-31), across all five approval flows.** `approval_workflow_steps` (org-configurable, ordered; multiple rows at the same step number are alternative approvers — any one suffices) plus `approval_instances` / `approval_instance_decisions` (runtime tracking and an append-only decision trail) form the generic engine — sequential multi-level, role- or relationship-based (a fixed role, the employee's reporting manager, their department manager, or one named person), generalized behind `core.is_eligible_request_approver()` / `core.approval_total_steps()`, both parameterized by `request_type`. Shipped in two passes: first wired to leave requests only as the reference implementation (`review_leave_request()`), then extended to loans (`review_loan`), expenses (`review_expense` — the `taxable` decision is only required and only ever written on the step that actually finalizes approval), overtime (`review_overtime_request` — same treatment for the rate multiplier), and leave encashment (`review_leave_encashment_request`, redeclared to route through the same gate). Backward compatible by construction, not a feature flag: an org with zero configured steps for a given `request_type` gets a single implicit step whose eligibility is byte-for-byte that type's prior hardcoded authorization (admin/hr_manager + manager/department-manager for leave; admin/payroll_manager/accountant for the other four, none of which ever had a manager-based fallback), so nothing changes for an org that never touches the Approval Workflows admin page. Loans/expenses/overtime previously had a broad direct-table UPDATE RLS policy alongside the RPC path — dropped in the same migration that wired them to the engine, since leaving it in place would have let anyone holding the base role bypass a configured multi-step workflow by updating the row directly (the same "no direct-update escape hatch" principle leave encashment was built with from day one). Also fixed in the same pass: the notification code for all five flows was only checking "did I just call approve," not "is this request now actually, fully approved" — a real bug for any org running a multi-step workflow, since an intermediate step's approval would have wrongly told the employee their request was decided. **Not built:** delegation, escalation, and SLA tracking — named in this same paragraph as part of "configurable," explicitly deferred. Parallel (as opposed to sequential) approval within a single level isn't modelled either; only the "any alternative approver at this step" shape exists.

### Employee lifecycle as an explicit spine

The source taxonomy's lifecycle model is a genuinely useful organising frame the current spec lacks:

```
Applicant → Offer → Onboarding → Probation → Confirmed →
Transfer/Promotion → Salary change → Performance → Exit → Final settlement → Offboarding
```

Plutus currently covers the tail (final settlement) and part of the middle. Modelling lifecycle **state transitions** explicitly — rather than as loose flags on the employee record — makes proration, contract expiry, probation tracking and offboarding fall out naturally instead of being bolted on.

**Employee-record scope: built (2026-09-12), deliberately not the full spine.** Scoped by explicit product decision to the post-hire employee record only — `Onboarding → Probation → Confirmed → (Suspended) → Offboarding → Exited` — leaving `Applicant → Offer` (recruitment's own separate `job_postings`/`candidates` pipeline) unmerged. A true full-spine version would need recruitment, `employees`, and `final_settlements` unified with real cross-module transitions (e.g. hiring a candidate auto-creating their employee record in a defined starting state) — a materially larger, riskier change than this pass, and still open.

What this pass actually adds: `getEmployeeLifecycleStage()` (`apps/wagebook/src/lib/format.ts`), a single derived stage computed from the flags that already existed and were each already independently correct (`employees.status`/`confirmed`/`probation_end_date`, the onboarding and offboarding checklist tables) — never a new stored column, deliberately, matching this codebase's own established convention for every other derived status (`getProbationStatus`, `getContractStatus`, `getPendingAgeTone`) of computing at read time rather than storing something that could drift from the source flags. Surfaced as an `EmployeeLifecycleStageBadge` on both the employee directory (with CSV export) and the View Employee page. A missing onboarding/offboarding checklist row — the normal case, since those rows are only lazily upserted on first edit — defaults to "done," not "still onboarding/offboarding forever," so an employee who joined before either checklist existed isn't retroactively mislabeled.

Not attempted: any new transition-history table for the probation → confirmed axis specifically (unlike `status`, which already has `employee_status_history` auto-logged via trigger) — this pass only unified the *read* side, and didn't add new audit logging beyond what already exists.

### Onboarding and offboarding process

Enrollment currently means one form. Real onboarding is a checklist with states: ~~documentation~~, ~~contract signing~~, probation tracking, confirmation. Offboarding likewise: ~~notice period~~, ~~exit checklist~~, ~~asset return~~, ~~**access revocation**~~, ~~clearance~~, ~~final settlement~~, ~~experience letter~~. **Built:** access revocation (the app layout gate reads the linked employee's `status` on every request — not a cached session claim — and redirects to `/account-revoked` the instant it's `terminated`, regardless of `org_membership` role; the page re-checks live on load too, so a reinstated employee isn't stuck behind a stale gate); an `employee_offboarding_checklist` covering the remaining manual offboarding steps — notice period served, assets returned, clearance obtained, experience letter issued — as one admin/HR-editable row per terminated employee on the edit page, shown alongside read-only indicators for the two steps that were already automatic or tracked elsewhere (access revoked, final settlement processed); and the onboarding-side counterpart, `employee_onboarding_checklist` (documentation collected, contract signed), shown for any non-terminated employee. "Documentation collected" was originally a bare attestation, unlinked to the employee documents repository sitting right above it on the same page — an admin could tick it with zero files on file. It's now enforced both ways: the Server Action checks first (for a clean error), and a `core.check_documentation_collected()` trigger on `employee_onboarding_checklist` is the backstop, requiring at least one `employee_documents` row for that employee before the flag can be set true; a companion trigger on `employee_documents` resets the flag back to false if every document for that employee is later deleted, the same "reset when the underlying condition changes" pattern as the contract/probation notified-at columns. "Contract signed" has no equivalent linkage — it's still a bare attestation, since a signed contract isn't necessarily one of the uploaded documents. Probation tracking and confirmation are handled separately by the existing `probation_end_date`/`confirmed` fields, not by either checklist. Neither checklist generates, templates or e-signs a document — both only track whether a step happened. Final settlement itself is a separate, longer-standing live module (prorated final-period pay, leave payout, gratuity and full loan clearance in one off-cycle run) — not new this round, just missing its strikethrough here until now.

### ~~Notifications and alerting~~

Deadline alerting is already a stated product requirement, but there's no notification infrastructure to deliver it. Needs email, in-app, and scheduled reminders, covering statutory deadlines, contract and probation expiry, approvals pending, and payroll events. **Built, all four categories:** contract and probation expiry, both in-app and scheduled, plus email delivery for every notification. Employee-lifecycle deadlines were already surfaced as page-load-computed badges (Expired/Ends soon on the directory) but never actually delivered as a Notification. `notifyLifecycleDeadlines` (checked on employees-list page load) notifies admin/hr_manager once per approaching contract-end or probation-end date within the same 14-day "ends soon" window the badges already used; idempotency is via two nullable `*_notified_at` columns on `employees`, reset by a database trigger whenever the underlying date changes, so extending a contract or probation date re-arms the alert rather than leaving it permanently silenced. `core.check_lifecycle_deadlines()` is the set-based, all-orgs SQL twin of that same idempotent check, now run independently of any page load by a real background job runner — `pg_cron`, scheduled daily at 06:00 UTC — the first thing in this app that runs on its own rather than in response to a request. Every notification insert, of any type, now also fires `core.send_notification_email()`, an `AFTER INSERT` trigger that calls Resend directly from Postgres via `pg_net` (no separate Edge Function to deploy), reading the API key, from-address, and app base URL from Supabase Vault by name — never from a migration file — and updating `notifications.emailed_at` once dispatched. Verified live to no-op silently (insert succeeds, `emailed_at` stays null, nothing throws) when those Vault secrets are absent, which is this build's actual current state — no Resend account has been connected yet, so today this is still in-app only in practice, but the delivery path itself is real and just needs an operator to run three `vault.create_secret(...)` calls. `core.check_pending_approvals()` closes the approvals-pending piece specifically: a second daily pg_cron job (staggered 30 minutes after the lifecycle check) reminds whoever actually approves each request type — admin/payroll_manager for loans, expenses, overtime requests and leave encashment; admin/hr_manager for leave requests, since that table's RLS grants a different role pair — once a request has sat in `status = 'pending'` for 3+ calendar days. Idempotency here can't reuse the `*_notified_at`-column trick (the same pattern is shared across five different tables, and adding a column to all five just for this felt like the wrong shape), so instead there's a small generic tracking table, `core.approval_aging_notified`, keyed by `(request_table, request_id)` — never exposed to PostgREST, only ever touched by the SECURITY DEFINER function itself. One reminder per request, ever, regardless of how much longer it stays pending. Verified live against a real fixture (a loan backdated 4 days, notified, tracking row created; rerunning the function produced no second notification). **Statutory-deadline alerts: built** (`20260812010001_statutory_deadline_reminders.sql`, extended by `20260816010000_itf_assessment_and_deadline_reminders.sql` for ITF) — a daily cron reminder for each of PAYE/pension/NHF/NSITF, reusing the exact remittance-day figures already encoded as account labels rather than inventing new ones; pension/NHF's real payment-triggered deadlines are approximated to a fixed calendar day, a disclosed simplification, not a gap. **Payroll-event notifications: built** — `pay_run_created` notifies whoever needs to review a new run before it posts.

### Payroll accounting depth

`packages/ledger` handles double-entry. Cost-centre / department / branch / project allocation, accrued payroll, and payroll liability tracking are what make the accounting integration genuinely useful to a finance team rather than a raw journal dump.

**Department cost-centre allocation: built, in two layers.** A single pay run's own GL export (`/payroll/[id]/export`) already breaks every posting down by employee and department (`ledger_postings` joined through `employees(departments(name))`) — a "Department (Cost Centre)" column sitting alongside account code and employee on every row. **Built (2026-09-12):** the cross-run rollup this was missing — `/reports` now aggregates gross payroll cost by department across every posted run to date (via `posted_payslips`, same source and same "excludes draft/validated/reversed" guarantee as every other report on that page), CSV-exportable, mirroring the existing PAYE-by-state pattern exactly. An employee with no department assigned rolls up under "Unassigned" rather than being silently dropped. **Not built:** branch-level allocation (branches exist as a work-location record but nothing rolls payroll cost up by branch), project allocation (no project entity exists anywhere in this build), accrued payroll (a period-end "payroll cost incurred but not yet paid" concept — today's `net_pay_payable` posts and presumably clears in the same cycle, with no accrual/reversal pair modelled), and any general ledger accounting-software sync (QuickBooks/Xero/Sage) — the export route's own comment already discloses this as a manual CSV, not an API integration.

### ~~Document generation and e-signature~~

~~Salary certificates~~, ~~employment letters~~, ~~contracts~~, tax documents. High-frequency HR requests, and templated generation is cheap relative to the goodwill it buys. **Built:** employment & salary certificate, an Offer/Confirmation Letter, and a Contract of Employment — all printable HTML documents (browser print-to-PDF, no PDF library dependency) generated on demand from live records, never a stored/stale copy. Admin/HR-facing at `/employees/[id]/certificate` (and equivalents), self-service at `/me/certificate` (and equivalents) — self-service always shows the employee's own real salary, since masking hides figures from *other* viewers, not from the employee themselves. The contract uses standard, generic terms drawn from what's on file, disclosed as not lawyer-reviewed language for a binding agreement. **The Contract of Employment now has real e-signature** (`EmploymentContract.tsx`): an in-house consent-and-audit-trail system, not a third-party provider — either party signs from the contract's own page after a confirm step, each signature pinned by a content hash so an edit to the underlying record (job grade, pay, dates, leave balance) invalidates the old signature as current and prompts a fresh one, while the original record is kept, never edited or deleted. This is a click-to-consent record (name, timestamp, IP, content hash), not a cryptographic or legally "qualified" e-signature under Nigeria's Evidence Act — disclosed as such on the printed contract. Tax documents beyond the annual reconciliation CSV/certificate (covered under Tax Management) remain unbuilt.

## 3. Deepens the existing moat

Extensions of things Plutus already does, aligned with the compliance-native positioning.

- ~~**Annual tax reconciliation and tax certificates**~~ — year-end reconciliation, employee tax certificates, annual returns. The natural completion of the compliance engine, and a strong renewal hook. **Built: both halves.** `/reports/annual` aggregates each employee's yearly gross/PAYE/pension/NHF/rent-relief across every non-reversed pay run (with CSV export), and a printable per-employee `AnnualTaxCertificate` — same shape as the employment/salary certificate — is generated from that data on demand, admin/HR-facing at `/employees/[id]/tax-certificate?year=` and self-service at `/me/tax-certificate?year=`. No PDF library, browser print-to-PDF only, no stored/stale copy. Annual returns (the actual NRS filing) and any e-filing integration remain unbuilt.
- **Government filing portal integrations** — direct submission to state IRS portals, PenCom, FMBN. Deepens the moat considerably; also the hardest integration work.
- ~~**Payroll variance and anomaly detection**~~ — flag month-over-month anomalies before a run is approved. A natural fit for the "correct by construction" thesis: catching a fat-fingered salary before it's paid is worth more than reporting it after. **Built, and upgraded to a real pre-approval gate.** Originally shipped (2026-07-24) as an informational-only, computed-on-page-load check, written before the draft/approval state model existed. Now (2026-07-31) persisted server-side: `core.detect_pay_run_variance()` runs inside `create_pay_run()` for every regular-frequency run (weekly/biweekly/monthly only — bonus/13th-month/arrears/final-settlement stay excluded, inherently one-off) and writes rows to `pay_run_variance_flags` — gross moved ≥30% vs the employee's most recent prior *posted* payslip of the same frequency (via the existing `posted_payslips` view), or an employee paid in the immediately-preceding run who's still active but missing from this one. `validate_pay_run()` (the draft → validated gate — see "Payroll locking and finalisation" above; this function was `approve_pay_run()` prior to 2026-09-12) now actually refuses to validate a draft with unacknowledged flags unless the caller explicitly passes `p_acknowledge_variance = true` (which stamps every flag as reviewed in the same transaction) — a real block, not just a discard-and-rerun prompt. For an already-posted run (created without going through the draft gate), flags still record and display, informational only, same as before; reversal remains the correction path there. Threshold moved from 25% to 30% and a second check (missing employee) was added in the same pass — both disclosed, adjustable constants, not statutory figures.
- ~~**Payroll register and reconciliation reports**~~ — standard finance-team expectations. **Built:** `/reports/register` — a per-run payroll register with a ledger-balanced reconciliation check for every pay run, including reversals.
- ~~**Bank account validation**~~ — validate account/NUBAN details before disbursement rather than discovering failures in a returned batch. **Built:** the CBN's NUBAN check-digit algorithm (`packages/compliance/src/nuban.ts`, golden-tested against a published worked example) runs against a curated list of ~25 major Nigerian banks and their 3-digit CBN/NIP codes; selecting a listed bank and entering a mistyped account number is rejected at save time on both the add and edit employee forms. An unlisted bank uses "Other" free text, which still enforces the 10-digit format but skips the checksum (no code to validate against). This confirms internal consistency of the number only — it is not a live account-name lookup against the bank, which stays unbuilt (Integrations' bank disbursement toggle remains an explicit demo).
- **Failed payment tracking and reconciliation.** Corrected framing: disbursement files were *not* generated at all prior to 2026-09-12, despite this line and the pay-run-locking migration's own comment assuming they were — `direct_deposit_payments` in feature-modules.ts was honestly marked `roadmap`, not `partial`, and that was the accurate signal. **Built (2026-09-12), the file-generation half:** a locked pay run's detail page can now export a disbursement file (CSV: employee, bank, account number, account name, amount, narration, and a flag for any employee missing bank details) — a bookkeeper-facing payment instruction list, not any bank's official bulk-upload format and not a transfer integration. `mark_pay_run_paid()` (see "Payroll locking and finalisation") tracks confirmed disbursement at the whole-run level. **Not built:** per-employee settlement tracking — nothing records whether an *individual* line in that exported file actually landed or bounced once sent to the bank; today "paid" is one flag for the whole run, not a reconciliation loop against actual transfer outcomes. That per-line tracking is the piece still genuinely open here.
- ~~**Attendance → payroll deduction rules**~~ — the prototype ties attendance to payroll visually; the deduction rules themselves need specifying. **Built:** unrecorded absences in the weekly Present/Late/Absent grid deduct pay automatically at pay-run creation, using the same daily-rate convention as unpaid leave, overtime and new-hire proration, combined into the run's single cumulative-PAYE calculation.
- **Multi-currency** — required for the pan-African roadmap. `packages/ledger` already resolves exponent per currency; don't let NGN assumptions leak into the core.

## 5. Not doing

Explicit rejections, recorded so they don't get re-litigated:

- **Biometric, RFID and facial-recognition attendance.** Hardware integration, a support burden, and unrelated to compliance. Integrate with existing devices later if a customer demands it; don't own the hardware problem.
- **Generic multi-country "tax engines" as a configuration surface.** Plutus's moat is *maintained, correct* rule sets, not a toolkit where customers configure their own tax rules. Configurable tax logic is how you sell inaccuracy as flexibility. Each country gets a curated rule set, authored and maintained by Plutus.
- **Schema-per-tenant multi-tenancy.** Already decided: Supabase with RLS row-level isolation. Don't reopen it.
- **Django / NestJS backends.** The source taxonomies suggest these. Plutus is Next.js + Supabase, and Django is rejected for three specific reasons, not by default:
  1. **It fights RLS.** Tenant isolation lives in Postgres policies keyed on `auth.uid()` from Supabase Auth. Django connects as one privileged user and expects to be the authority on permissions, so the path of least resistance moves the security boundary out of the database — the wrong direction when a leak means one employer seeing another's salaries.
  2. **It risks a second compliance engine.** `packages/compliance` is TypeScript. A Django backend either reimplements PAYE in Python — two implementations, drifting, golden tests covering one — or crosses a language boundary per payslip.
  3. **Its main advantage doesn't apply.** Django Admin's value is auto-generated back-office CRUD. Plutus's HR screens are customer-facing surfaces styled with the Ledger system; you would never ship Django Admin to a client. And pointed at customer data it bypasses the RLS doing tenant isolation, so the one feature that justifies Django is the one that breaks the security model.

  What replaces the real need: **Supabase Studio** for internal data inspection, `supabase gen types typescript` so the CRUD layer stays schema-synced rather than hand-written, and **shadcn/ui + react-hook-form + zod** for the form-heavy HR surface.

  **Still open, and a separate decision:** the stack lists a Python worker on Railway running "batch payroll runs" while the compliance engine is TypeScript — those cannot both be true without duplicating the engine. Resolve by running batch in a Node worker (cleanest, engine stays in one language), moving the engine to Python as a service, or dropping the Python tier until something genuinely needs it. Rejecting Django does not resolve this.
- **Corporate card integration, travel booking, flight/hotel APIs.** Far outside the wedge.

## 6. Open strategic questions

Flagging rather than resolving, since these are business decisions.

### Kuwait / GCC — the notable divergence

The original `business-platform` scope for Wagebook was **Nigeria + Kuwait/GCC** (PIFSS, indemnity, WPS). The Plutus prototype and investor brief drop Kuwait entirely for Nigeria → Ghana → Kenya. The uploaded taxonomy's **Visa, Work Permit & Government Documents** module is explicitly framed as GCC-critical — passport, visa, work permit, residence permit, Civil ID, expiry tracking, sponsor information, government reporting.

Whether that module matters depends on a decision that hasn't been recorded anywhere: **is Kuwait/GCC still in scope, or was it superseded by the pan-African thesis?** The two markets pull the product in different directions — GCC needs heavy visa/sponsorship document tracking and end-of-service indemnity; West/East Africa needs multi-state tax authority routing. Both are buildable, but not simultaneously at this stage, and the answer changes what phase 7 looks like.

Worth noting the pan-African story is also the stronger investor narrative, since it turns one reform into a repeatable rules-layer play. If Kuwait is being kept warm for personal or commercial reasons, that's legitimate — but record it as a decision rather than leaving two roadmaps in circulation.

### Others

- **Contractor/gig workforce depth.** WHT is handled, but full contractor management (onboarding, invoicing, 1099-equivalent reporting) is unscoped. Nigeria's gig economy makes this a plausible wedge extension.
- **Employee count sweet spot.** Whether Plutus targets SMEs (20–100 employees, self-serve, low touch) or mid-market/enterprise (500+, implementation-led) changes almost everything downstream — pricing, onboarding, integration depth, support model. The investor brief implies mid-market/enterprise; the product's self-serve setup flow implies SME.
- **Payroll bureau / accountant channel.** Nigerian SMEs often outsource payroll to accounting firms. A multi-client bureau view could be a faster distribution channel than direct sales — and would need explicit multi-org switching that the current single-org model doesn't have.
